    01 · Why this, why you

    The expensive part is figuring out what went wrong.

    Incident replay

    Something odd happens in a test. Weeks later someone needs the minutes around it, lined up across code logs, sensor streams and video that live on different machines in different formats.

    Thin links

    Field vehicles and customer sites rarely have bandwidth to spare. The useful move is to send the summary first and leave the raw data where it was recorded.

    Field triage

    When a unit breaks on site, the person there often doesn't know the internals and calls HQ. A small team ends up doing engineering, support and field service at once.

    What we heard across our discovery calls with robotics engineers and operators, October 2026. Summarized at a general level.

    Where it could fit at Boston Engineering
    • Unmanned systems. Underwater and field vehicles like BIOSwimmer log on board and come back to a dock with a thin link.
    • Connected products + AR. IIoT telemetry and AR-guided service already run through your practice. A first answer could land in the tech's headset.
    • Regulated programs. Defense and medical work means data stays inside program boundaries. The mesh respects them rather than asking you to move data out.
    • Senior engineer time. Less time hunting for a CAN bitrate, more time on the problems you were hired for.

    "Bring the agent to the data, not the data to the agent."

    Our working thesis. We want to hear where it breaks for you.
    02 · What runs where

    Install a worker. It joins the graph.

    1. 1

      Pick a master node

      The supervisor runs on a server or workstation inside your network. It plans, routes and keeps the capability graph. It never executes jobs itself.

    2. 2

      Add headed devices

      Workstations, rig PCs, Jetsons, Raspberry Pis and laptops run the full worker. Start with three. Linux and macOS first, Windows when a partner needs it.

    3. 3

      Reach headless devices

      MCUs, PLCs, power supplies, scopes and NAS boxes are reached through a nearby worker over serial, CAN, Modbus, SCPI or SSH. Nothing new is installed on them.

    4. 4

      Set permissions per node

      Each node gets standing ceilings for read, write, exec and actuate. Layer sites under sub-supervisors. Disconnected components, like an enclave, run their own mesh.

    03 · Security model

    The model proposes. Policy decides. You approve anything physical.

    Three identities

    Node: which machine. Agent: which worker is acting. Lease: what this one job may touch, for how long. No standing root access anywhere.

    Deterministic policy

    A policy engine, not the LLM, checks access lists and issues every lease. A model can ask for more. It can't grant itself more.

    Read-only first

    The first four weeks of a pilot are read-only. After that, writes are scoped and snapshotted, and anything that moves hardware waits for a named person.

    Data stays put

    Workers return summaries and handles, not bulk copies. Raw logs, sonar and video stay on the machine that recorded them.

    Air-gap ready

    A disconnected enclave runs its own supervisor with a local model. There is no bridge to it from outside, and the console shows that plainly.

    Audit + kill switch

    Every lease, action, result and model call is logged append-only and exportable to your SIEM. One switch revokes every lease at once.

    Plainly

    We're an early team and we don't hold certifications yet. So the mesh is built to run inside the boundaries and controls you already operate, using your network, your accounts and your logs. Encrypted transport (WireGuard-style), mutual node identity, and nothing leaving your network unless you allow it.

    05 · A low-risk first step

    Twelve weeks. Three machines. One real incident.

    Week 0

    Install + agree metrics

    Three machines you pick. Success metrics written down on day one.

    Weeks 1–4

    Read-only triage

    Replay one real past incident. Search across machines. No writes, no motion.

    Weeks 5–8

    Replays on the right box

    Reruns on the right GPU. Scoped writes with snapshots, only if you want them.

    Weeks 9–12

    Measure + decide

    Time from failure to first answer, investigations closed, engineer hours back.

    What we'd ask of you
    • A champion engineer who owns the pilot
    • 30 minutes a week of feedback
    • One past incident we can replay in week 1
    What we'd love to learn on the call
    • When a vehicle misbehaves in a test, how long until someone knows why?
    • Where do logs, telemetry and video live today, and in what formats?
    • What can't leave which network, and who signs off?
    • Who gets the call when a field tech is stuck?
    • Which workflow would you trust us with first?
